— About

A multi-decade vantage on how real companies and technologies get built.

“Europe’s measured virtues are an underappreciated strategic asset. The challenge is translating its talent and research into globally competitive companies — particularly in deep tech, AI, and the technologies that will define the next economy.”

Dimitar is an entrepreneur and investment professional whose career spans the full lifecycle of innovation — from concept to global market leadership. His operating experience includes building CWP Renewables, which became the largest renewable energy company in Australia, developing Europe’s largest onshore wind farm, and raising more than $4 billion in project finance and equity.

As Managing Partner at PostScriptum Ventures and an active angel investor, he backs high-conviction deep tech businesses across AI and Industry 4.0, advanced manufacturing, applied robotics, biotech, electrification, and frontier hardware. The thesis is consistent: technologies with genuine moats, building real things in the real world, with founders capable of operating at global scale.

He serves as a Board Member of the Bulgarian Venture Capital Association, Co-Chair of the AmCham Bulgaria Energy Committee, and a Member of the European Council on Foreign Relations. He has been a keynote speaker at COP26, COP28, the World Hydrogen Congress, the Ukraine Recovery Conference, and the Dii Leadership Summit, among others.

Dimitar holds a B.A. in Economics from Swarthmore College (with minors in Computer Science and Mathematics) and completed a year at the London School of Economics. He is based between Sofia and London.

— Investment Philosophy

Backing builders of consequential things.

I look for technologies with real moats — physical, scientific, or systems-level — being built by founders willing to take on the difficult, capital-intensive, multi-year work that creates durable companies. Software is necessary but rarely sufficient. The interesting frontier sits where AI, advanced hardware, and deep domain expertise compound into businesses that reshape industries.

A second conviction runs underneath the first: a better planet and a stronger economy are built from the same materials. Two decades operating inside the energy transition — realizing more than 2GW of renewables, pioneering green hydrogen at gigawatt scale, advancing 7GW across Southeast Europe — has shown me that real decarbonization is hard engineering, scalable manufacturing, and the disciplined deployment of capital. The technologies that will reshape the next economy are the same ones that will sustain the next century. Profit and planet are not in tension.

— 01
Hard problems, real moats

Bias toward technical depth and engineering complexity over pure software plays. The kinds of companies where execution alone is a competitive advantage and reproduction is genuinely difficult.

— 02
Founders who can scale

Strategic vision plus operational stamina. Leaders who can navigate complex regulation, secure substantial capital, and conquer international markets — not just optimize a funnel.

— 03
Active partnership

Capital is necessary but rarely the constraint. Where my expertise compounds the financial contribution — opening markets, refining models, recruiting — is where I want to lean in.

— Philanthropy

Investing in the next generation.

Fund Compass

Fund Compass is a Bulgarian philanthropic initiative making world-class international education accessible to talented Bulgarian students. The fund underwrites scholarships, mentorship, and admissions guidance for promising young people pursuing degrees at top universities abroad — investing in the human capital that will shape the country’s next chapter.

Education was the single biggest lever in my own life. Fund Compass exists so that the same opportunity is no longer determined by the economic circumstances a student happens to be born into.
